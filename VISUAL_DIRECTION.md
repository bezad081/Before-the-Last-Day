# Visual Direction Guide — Before the Last Day

## North star

Use **Zom 100: Bucket List of the Dead** as a recurring visual reference for energy, color, expressive silhouettes, urban scale, and the contrast between a bright world and frightening events. The game must remain its own story and visual identity: do not trace screenshots, recreate exact shots, or copy the anime's named characters, outfits, logos, or compositions.

Reference hub: https://zom100.com/en/

## What to borrow from the visual language

- **Color contrast:** clear cyan/blue skies, sunlit warm yellows and peach, saturated greens, coral/pink accents, and deep blue-green shadows. Avoid letting the whole screen collapse into muted navy and grey.
- **Lighting:** strong morning sunlight, readable highlights, soft atmospheric depth, and occasional warm rim light on the protagonist.
- **City design:** layered streets, dense but legible buildings, varied shop fronts, signs, windows, overhead wires, road markings, street furniture, and objects that tell a story. Use a believable Japanese-inspired contemporary city as a broad reference, but invent the exact buildings and signage.
- **Graphic accents:** controlled splashes of bright color, painted posters, vivid shop awnings, and bold shapes. These should guide the eye toward meaningful spaces rather than decorate every empty patch.
- **Character readability:** an original protagonist with a strong silhouette, spiky dark hair, a green outer layer, a distinctive backpack, dark trousers, and a small high-visibility accent. Keep proportions and outfit details original; use clear separation between head, torso, arms, legs, and shoes.
- **Emotional contrast:** cheerful daylight and lively color should make abandoned streets, missing crowds, sirens, and unsettling clues feel more disturbing—not turn every scene into a comedy.
- **Motion:** readable anticipation, foot contact, acceleration, landing, head/torso response, and small secondary motion. Camera framing should preserve the sense of speed without hiding interactable clues.

## Scene-by-scene visual checklist

For every new scene or chapter, record:
1. **Reference moment:** the type of shot or atmosphere being studied (e.g. bright street wide shot, tense interior, sunset pause).
2. **Palette:** dominant light, shadow, and accent colors.
3. **Architecture:** building shapes, signage, street furniture, and background depth.
4. **Character staging:** silhouette, expression, pose, and how the character reads against the background.
5. **Environmental story:** at least three details that imply what happened before the player arrived.
6. **Gameplay readability:** ground plane, hazards, exits, interactables, and dialogue space must remain clear.
7. **Originality check:** translate the reference into new shapes and staging; do not reproduce a frame or character design directly.

## Current implementation status

The first pass introduces a bright cyan-to-peach morning sky, layered colorful skyline, stronger facade/awning accents, road paint splashes, and a green-jacketed original protagonist with teal backpack and bright shoes. This is a **palette and mood pass**, not yet a complete anime-quality art pass.

## Next visual milestones

1. Replace repeated flat facade patterns with district-specific architecture and more convincing perspective layers.
2. Upgrade the protagonist's face, hair silhouette, clothing folds, and animation poses without copying Akira's exact design.
3. Add animated environmental life: wind, loose paper, sign sway, flickering lights, distant movement, and scene-specific weather.
4. Improve shot composition and camera staging for story beats, discoveries, and sudden events.
5. Add original background illustrations or sprite assets where they provide more value than procedural Graphics shapes.
6. Review each milestone against this guide on desktop and mobile, then adjust contrast and readability.

## Quality rule

**Colorful does not mean cluttered.** Reserve the strongest saturation for the protagonist, interactable clues, story events, and selected architectural accents. The player should always understand where to move, what can be inspected, and what has changed in the scene.
